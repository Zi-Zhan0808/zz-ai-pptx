# Prose: removing template-speak without damaging meaning

Academic decks have a specific failure mode: they do not sound *cheap*, they
sound **generic** — the same opener, the same hedges, the same empty conclusion
as every other deck, so the actual finding becomes invisible.

The rule in one line: **humanity comes from judgement and precise hedging, not
from casual phrasing.** Slang on a slide reads as unserious; vagueness reads as
evasive. Neither is "human".

## What to cut

| Pattern | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| 「近年来，随着……的快速发展」 | zero information; delays the claim | the claim itself |
| 「在当今……时代/背景下」 | same | a number or a finding |
| 深刻变革 / 全面赋能 / 有效提升 | 空转大词, unmeasurable | the measured effect |
| 「具有重要意义」「具有重要价值」 | asserts importance instead of showing it | why it changes a decision |
| 「由于时间有限，本文仅……」 | apologises for the artefact | say what the scope *is*, once, plainly |
| 「未来可进一步研究」 | closes nothing | one concrete next experiment |
| 「众所周知」「毋庸置疑」 | claims consensus without a citation | cite it, or drop it |
| 「综上所述，本文……」 | template收束 | the actual takeaway |

## What to keep — these are not sloppiness

Rewriting must never remove:

- **Meaningful hedges**: 「在 X 条件下」「体外模型中」「统计上不显著」「与……一致但并不排除」
  — deleting these turns a careful claim into an overclaim, which is a factual
  error, not a style improvement.
- **Precise terms**: 「转录后调控」 must not become 「调控」;「效应量」 must not become
  「影响」. Terminology carries the argument.
- **Author stance**: 「我认为这一解释更强，因为……」 is exactly the "human" quality
  wanted. Do not neutralise opinion into summary.
- **Numbers, units, sample sizes, p-values** — verbatim.

## Judgement beats decoration

The difference a reader feels between a generic deck and a sharp one is almost
always **stance**, not vocabulary:

| Generic | Sharper |
|---|---|
| 「METTL3 在 hTSC 中发挥重要作用」 | 「METTL3 缺失后，身份基因先于分化标志下降，因果方向更可能是 METTL3 → 身份」 |
| 「结果与文献一致」 | 「与 [2] 一致；但 [2] 用的是过表达体系，本研究的敲低结果方向相反，值得追查」 |
| 「未来可进一步研究机制」 | 「下一步只需做一个回补实验：若身份基因部分恢复，则机制成立」 |
| 「存在一定局限性」 | 「主要局限是缺少体内验证，因此结论目前只适用于体外模型」 |

Note what the right column does: it names a direction, a conflict, a test, a
scope. Each could be wrong — which is what makes it a claim worth hearing.

## Structure the argument, not the topic

- **Titles state claims.** A slide titled 「实验结果」 forces the audience to work
  out why it is there.
- **One idea per slide.** If you need "and" to describe the slide, split it.
- **Evidence before interpretation is not always right.** Lead with the claim,
  then the evidence, then the caveat. A briefing is not a mystery novel.
- **End on a decision or a question**, not on thanks alone.

## Speaker notes are a different register

On the slide: terse. In the notes: the talk track.

- Slide: 「3.2× 下调」 / Notes: 「这里强调是 qPCR 的独立重复，不是同一批样本的再分析，所以我不认为这是批次效应。」
- Notes may state what you are unsure about — that is where honesty is cheap and
  credibility is earned.
- Do not put full sentences on the slide and the same sentences in the notes;
  one of them is wasted.

## A quick self-check before delivering

Read each title in sequence, alone. You should be able to follow the argument
without the body text.

If a title could belong to any other paper on the topic, rewrite it.

If a limitation slide does not narrow the claim, it is decoration.

If you deleted every 「重要」 and 「关键」, would any sentence lose information? If
not, delete them.
